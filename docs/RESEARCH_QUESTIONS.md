# Research questions in the submitted dissertation

The submitted dissertation framed the work around five questions:

1. What research gaps exist in the solar-flare prediction literature?
2. What datasets are available for solar-flare prediction, and which are accessible and suitable for the study?
3. Which SHARP parameters are most significant for solar-flare prediction?
4. Which machine-learning approaches and algorithms are most suitable for the chosen dataset in light of previous work?
5. Which validation techniques can be used to assess the developed models reliably?

The dissertation's practical aim was to identify a suitable predictive machine-learning approach for a 24-hour solar-flare forecasting setting using B, C, M and X flare classes.

## Variable-selection focus

The literature review highlighted six SHARP parameters as especially important in prior work:

- Total Unsigned Current Helicity (TOTUSJH)
- Total Unsigned Vertical Current (TOTUSJZ)
- Total Unsigned Flux (USFLUX)
- Absolute Value of the Net Current Helicity (ABSNJZH)
- Total Photospheric Magnetic Free Energy Density (TOTPOT)
- Mean Characteristic Twist Parameter, alpha (MEANALP)

The selected dataset contained 13 SHARP parameters. The dissertation notes that MEANALP was not included in that dataset.
